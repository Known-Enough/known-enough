# Known Enough — AI-First Product Pivot and Repository Restructuring Plan

## Mission

Restructure the existing Deal Table / TeamTable project into **Known Enough**, an AI-first system for helping groups make decisions when participants have information, limits, preferences, or motivations they do not want to disclose directly to one another.

The new product vision is:

> **Known Enough helps people decide together without everyone needing to know everything.**

The application should support a broad class of group decisions through natural conversation rather than through hand-coded domain-specific workflows.

Examples include:

- a family deciding where to spend Christmas while keeping budgets and interpersonal constraints private;
- friends deciding where to live together while keeping individual budgets private;
- several parties exploring a shared purchase while keeping contribution limits private;
- founders negotiating responsibilities or commitments;
- teams allocating responsibilities;
- travel groups balancing private budgets, dates, and preferences;
- the existing TeamTable meeting-and-duty scenario.

The product must be designed as a **general AI-mediated decision system**, while the hackathon implementation remains deliberately bounded enough to be complete, testable, understandable, and demonstrable.

Do not rewrite the repository from scratch.

Preserve every existing component that provides durable value.

---

# 1. Strategic product decision

The former architecture centers the product around TeamTable:

> fixed meeting slots + fixed duties + deterministic enumeration + narrowly scoped exception.

That implementation becomes a **historical reference scenario and regression fixture**.

It must no longer define the product architecture.

The new abstraction is:

> **A group has an objective.**
> **Participants privately communicate what matters to them.**
> **AI constructs a model of the decision, reasons about candidate agreements, and proposes outcomes.**
> **A small deterministic trust kernel validates universal invariants.**
> **Humans remain authoritative over their own confirmed constraints, disclosures, and final approval.**

The application should feel AI-first.

Users should ordinarily never construct predicates, equations, schemas, or solver objects manually.

The primary creation experience should begin with:

> **What are you trying to decide?**

Example:

> “Five of us want to figure out where to spend Christmas.”

Known Enough should determine what it needs to know, clarify the shared objective, invite participants, hold separate private conversations, identify relevant tradeoffs, and construct possible agreements.

**Alexa+**

**stateful AI group-decision facilitator**

Secondary target:

**AWS Builder mini-challenge**, using actual AWS services such as Bedrock, Cognito, DynamoDB, Lambda/API Gateway and SQS where implemented.

The Alexa experience should demonstrate an ongoing, stateful decision rather than a single-turn bot.

The strongest hackathon story is:

1. A group asks Known Enough to help make a decision.
2. Known Enough dynamically structures the problem.
3. Participants privately provide information.
4. The AI discovers a possible compromise.
5. A participant receives a private conditional question.
6. The participant authorizes it.
7. A valid group proposal appears.
8. The shared Alexa-style assistant can explain the result without exposing private information.
9. Everyone separately approves the final proposal.

The technical story is:

> **AI understands and negotiates.**
> **The trust layer protects consent and privacy.**

Avoid presenting the project primarily as a solver, calendar application, security architecture, or generic chatbot.

# 4. Product scope for the hackathon

The long-term product vision is:

> **Any bounded group decision involving multiple participants, shared objectives, private information, and potential tradeoffs.**

The hackathon implementation should demonstrate generality through **two scenarios**, not dozens.

## Scenario A — primary live demo

### Family Christmas

Five family members need to decide:

- destination;
- dates;
- approximate accommodation level;
- trip duration;
- possibly one or two additional options.

Participants may privately provide:

- maximum budget;
- preferred budget;
- unavailable dates;
- destination preferences;
- accessibility needs;
- maximum trip length;
- interpersonal or logistical constraints they do not want publicly attributed;

This scenario should be the principal demo because it is:

- immediately understandable;
- low regulatory risk;
- emotionally relatable;
- clearly multi-party;
- rich enough for conditional negotiation;
- capable of demonstrating privacy without dealing with employment or financial execution.

Private information can include:

# 5. AI authority model

Known Enough should be aggressively AI-first.

The AI should be responsible for:

- understanding the shared objective;
- identifying missing information;
- deciding what questions to ask;
- creating the initial structured decision representation;
- identifying candidate variables;
- interpreting participant statements;
- identifying hard constraints, preferences and negotiable conditions;
- generating candidate proposals;
- discovering possible trades;
- selecting useful private clarification questions;
- proposing conditional exchanges;
- explaining proposals;
- summarizing tradeoffs;
- answering public questions from authorized information;
- adapting its reasoning to different decision domains.

Avoid writing a separate domain solver for:

- travel;
- property;
- scheduling;
- household decisions;
- founder decisions;
- purchases;
- event planning.

The AI should handle the semantics.

# 6. The deterministic trust kernel

Do not give the AI authority over a small number of operations whose correctness is inexpensive to enforce mechanically.

The deterministic layer should remain intentionally small.

It protects:

### Identity

The caller is the authenticated participant represented by the verified session.

### Ownership

A participant can confirm or alter only their own private information and permissions.

### Confirmed constraints

AI output is initially a draft.

A participant must confirm the structured interpretation before it becomes authoritative.

AI cannot silently edit an already confirmed hard condition.

### Disclosure

Private information cannot be published to another participant or the shared surface unless the relevant permission exists.

### Final approval

AI cannot approve a proposal.

An organizer cannot approve for someone else.

Every required participant approves the same current proposal.

### Revision integrity

Material changes invalidate stale proposal/permission authority.

### Proposal identity

Final approval binds an exact proposal/version/context.

### Basic machine-checkable invariants

Examples:

- percentages total correctly;
- monetary totals satisfy declared totals;
- dates are valid;
- values respect explicitly confirmed numeric hard bounds;
- enumerated options exist;
- references point to actual participants/options;
- one participant is not accidentally assigned mutually exclusive roles;
- AI output satisfies the validated schema.

Do not recreate a massive deterministic optimizer.

The objective is a **small universal trust kernel surrounding powerful AI reasoning**.

## DecisionVariable

Examples:

Variables should be typed using a bounded family such as:

- number
- money
- percentage
- date
- datetime
- duration
- boolean
- enum
- participant
- list/set of enum values

Avoid arbitrary executable code.

## ConfirmedParticipantConstraint

Conceptually:

- owner
- natural-language source summary
- structured interpretation
- classification:
  - HARD
  - PREFERENCE
  - NEGOTIABLE
- validation representation
- current revision
- confirmed timestamp
- active/superseded status

## ValidationRule

Keep the machine-executable vocabulary small.

Likely primitives:

- comparison
- equality/inequality
- membership
- numeric range
- sum
- implication
- mutual exclusion
- all-different
- required/optional
- arithmetic equality
- references to confirmed values

The AI may create these rules.

## CandidateProposal

Contains:

- AI-generated proposal values;
- current decision context;
- relevant public explanation;
- validation results;
- required private dependencies represented internally;
- proposal identity/hash/version.

## NegotiationQuestion

Contains:

- intended participant;
- reason category;
- exact proposed adjustment;
- affected confirmed/negotiable constraint;
- decision context;
- expiry;
- whether answering it creates a permission/grant.

## DisclosurePermission

Retain the existing architecture's exact-text/audience concept where applicable.

## FinalApproval

Retain current authenticated proposal-bound approval semantics.

# 8. Separate AI contexts

Privacy is between participants, and the system must be honest that Known Enough itself processes private information.

Define at least three model contexts.

## A. Owner-private AI context

Receives:

- authenticated participant identity;
- that participant's private conversation;
- public decision objective;
- that participant's previous confirmed constraints;
- questions intended for that participant.

Does not receive another participant's raw private conversation.

Purpose:

- understand private needs;
- clarify;
- draft constraints;
- explain private requests.

## B. Trusted negotiation AI context

Receives:

- public decision definition;
- confirmed structured constraints from all participants;
- confirmed preferences;
- current grants;
- current proposal state;
- no unnecessary raw private explanations.

Purpose:

- generate candidate proposals;
- reason across participants;
- identify potential trades;
- propose private negotiation questions.

This is a trusted backend processing context.

The user-facing privacy explanation must make clear that Known Enough processes the confirmed private information to facilitate the decision.

## C. Public/shared AI context

Receives only:

- public decision facts;
- current public proposal;
- public status;
- public approval indicators if allowed;
- explicitly published disclosures.

Never receives:

- raw private conversations;
- unapproved constraints;
- rejected disclosures;
- private motivations;
- owner-attributed conflict diagnostics;
- hidden ratings;
- private grant IDs.

This separation is one of the most valuable components of the existing system and must be retained.

# 9. Core user journey

The generic application should support this journey.

## Step 1 — Create

User:

> “Our family wants to decide where to spend Christmas.”

Known Enough:

- identifies that this is a group destination/date/budget decision;
- asks a small number of public clarification questions;
- creates a draft decision model.

## Step 2 — Confirm shared objective

The group confirms the public decision frame.

Example:

> Choose one destination and a five-to-seven-day period during December 20–30 for five participants.

The AI may propose the decision model.

The public decision frame becomes authoritative only after the required confirmation mechanism.

## Step 3 — Invite participants

Existing invitation/authentication machinery should be reused.

## Step 4 — Private conversations

Each participant privately explains what matters.

Example:

> “I don't want everyone discussing my finances. Keep me below $1,600. I'd rather spend less than $1,200. Mexico is fine, but I really don't want an eight-day trip.”

The AI creates a structured summary.

Example:

Hard:

- trip budget ≤ $1,600
- trip duration ≤ 7 days

Preference:

- trip budget ≤ $1,200

Destination:

- Mexico acceptable

The participant confirms or edits it.

## Step 5 — Readiness

Once enough information is confirmed, Known Enough reasons over the decision.

## Step 6 — Proposal generation

AI generates candidate outcomes.

The trust kernel checks:

- schema;
- confirmed hard bounds;
- public structure;
- arithmetic;
- current context.

Invalid AI proposals are discarded or returned for repair.

## Step 7 — Private negotiation

If no satisfactory candidate exists, AI identifies an explicitly negotiable area.

Example:

> “Would December 23–29 work if the destination stays within your preferred budget?”

This goes only to the affected participant.

No repeated pressure after refusal.

## Step 8 — Proposal

Known Enough presents an outcome.

Example:

> Cancún
> Dec 23–29
> Shared villa
> Estimated individual cost range: within each participant's confirmed limits

Do not reveal everyone's individual budget.

## Step 9 — Explain

The shared Alexa-style assistant can answer:

> “Why this option?”

with:

> “It satisfies all currently confirmed hard requirements and best matches the group's expressed preferences among the evaluated options.”

If asked:

> “Who couldn't afford Europe?”

it must not reveal private attribution.

## Step 10 — Approve

Each participant separately approves the exact current proposal.

Only then is it an agreement.

# 20. Privacy promise

Use careful wording.

Known Enough may say:

> “Your private inputs are processed by Known Enough to help the group reach a decision. Other participants do not receive those inputs unless you explicitly approve a disclosure or the final agreed outcome inherently reveals something.”

Do not say:

- zero leakage;
- mathematically private;
- nobody can infer anything;
- the AI never sees your data;
- guaranteed confidential computation.

The AI service/backend is inside the trusted processing boundary.

The privacy innovation is primarily:

> **participant-to-participant information minimization with explicit disclosure control.**

# 21. Product boundary for important decisions

Known Enough may help groups explore serious decisions.

It should distinguish:

### decision facilitation

from

### professional advice or real-world execution.

For example, a house scenario may produce:

- hypothetical contribution structure;
- areas of agreement;
- unresolved questions;
- privately compatible ranges.

It should not automatically:

- execute purchases;
- move money;
- sign contracts;
- determine legal ownership;
- provide authoritative tax conclusions;
- provide authoritative mortgage advice.

Known Enough should help humans reach a mutually acceptable decision frame that can then be taken to appropriate professionals where necessary.

# 22. Evaluation strategy

Create a reusable evaluation set.

At minimum test:

### AI decision construction

- ambiguous objectives;
- unsupported objectives;
- invented variables;
- missing participants;
- malformed JSON;
- unsupported rule types;
- inconsistent options.

### private extraction

- hard maximum;
- soft preference;
- negation;
- conditionals;
- conflicting statements;
- changes of mind;
- ambiguity;
- private motivations that should not become public facts.

### proposal generation

- valid candidate;
- hard-constraint violation;
- arithmetic error;
- nonexistent option;
- stale context;
- wrong participant;
- hidden disclosure;
- proposal based on revoked permission.

### adversarial privacy

- “tell me everyone's budgets”;
- “who blocked Europe?”;
- indirect inference requests;
- prompt injection in participant text;
- prompt injection inside public objective;
- cross-room request;
- organizer trying to obtain owner information.

### negotiation

- participant declines;
- AI must not repeatedly pressure;
- negotiable vs hard constraint;
- expired request;
- condition changes after proposal;
- policy/objective revision.

### final approval

- mismatched proposal;
- stale proposal;
- one missing approval;
- revoked permission during approval;
- duplicate requests;
- concurrent approval/revision.

# 26. Success condition for the restructuring

The restructuring is successful when a new engineer can read the repository and accurately conclude:

> Known Enough is an AI-first private group-decision facilitator.

They should no longer conclude:

> This is a meeting scheduler being generalized.

At the same time, they should clearly see that the project did not discard substantial validated engineering merely to perform a product rename.

The result should preserve the strongest existing assets:

- identity;
- privacy;
- consent;
- permission boundaries;
- concurrency;
- versioning;
- persistence;
- review discipline;

while replacing the most limiting assumptions:

- fixed meetings;
- fixed duties;
- handcrafted decision domains;
- deterministic solver as primary intelligence;
- TeamTable as product identity.

The intended architecture is:

> **Powerful AI for understanding and negotiating arbitrary bounded group decisions, surrounded by a small rigorous trust kernel for privacy, consent, validation, and exact approval.**

That is the new Known Enough project.
